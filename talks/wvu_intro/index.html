  • OHBWC (established 1912) is the largest state-run workers'-compensation system in the U.S.
  • In Ohio's manufacturing sector, contact with objects / equipment accounts for:
    • 34.85% of the sector's lost-time (LT) claims.
    • 57.11% of the sector's total claims (lost-time + medical-only).
  • LT claims = 8 or more days away from work; MO claims = medical care only or ≤ 7 days away.
Source: Bunting et al., J Safety Res. 2021 (open access) · OH Auditor of State FY24 BWC report.

Ohio's Manufacturing Industry: Safety Issues — Root Causes

  • Standardized machine guarding checklists are encouraged to identify hazards and system deficiencies, especially in metal-working and similar industries.
  • Temporary workers have higher injury rates involving contact with objects & equipment because of:
    • Less safety training relative to permanent staff.
    • Shorter job tenure and less familiarity with site-specific hazards.
  • Recognized interventions with documented evidence:
    • Machine guarding and safety saws.
    • PPE programs — safety glasses, gloves, and awareness campaigns.
    • NIOSH guidance on Hazardous Energy Control (Lockout/Tagout).
  • Yet training itself remains static, generic, and one-shot at onboarding — a clear opening for adaptive, immersive approaches.
Sources: Bunting JSR 2021 · Smith AJIM 2017 · Lombardi AJIM 2014 · NIOSH Hazardous Energy Control Resource Guide.

Proposing SIGHT: Safety Immersion & Gamified Hazard Training

Two innovations on top of static, generic, one-shot training
Innovation 1

Dynamic VR Training

Gamified, repeatable training modules that use generative AI to adapt scenarios, hazards, and feedback to each trainee's skill level and to real production conditions.

Innovation 2

AR Coaching on the Floor

Real-time, IoT-enhanced augmented-reality guidance for safety and productivity — using speech interfaces, vision, and live sensor data on the shop floor.

Targeted outcomes
  • Personalized, immersive hazard recognition and response.
  • Fewer cuts, lacerations, and crush incidents — the dominant LT-claim categories in OH.
  • A stronger empirical link between safety and productivity.
  • A scalable solution for manufacturers of all sizes across Ohio — not just our partner sites.

From Vision to Reality: Our Five-Phased Approach

Five phases — from baseline assessment and content design, through VR/AR prototyping and pilot deployment, to evaluation and dissemination across Ohio manufacturers.

Targeting · NSF IDSS (NSF 26-509) — Category II

AI + Predictive Maintenance — transitioning an OEM-anchored pilot data service to national scale
NSF 26-509 · Cat II

Our planned Category II project

An OEM-anchored, multi-company predictive-maintenance data service
  • Industry partner (OEM) contributes operational and sensor data across multiple manufacturing companies in their installed base — a uniquely broad fleet view.
  • AI methods focus on predictive maintenance: cross-company failure-mode learning, remaining-useful-life estimation, and anomaly detection at scale.
  • Category II is the right fit — we are transitioning a regional / pilot data system to national-scale operational quality, not building from scratch.
  • Outputs are open data services + reference AI models that downstream research groups (including HCIS, UB) can build on.
